Feeding tips for providers and families

Starting out

  • Most babies are ready for solids around 6 months: sitting up with support, good head control, and interest in food.
  • Follow each family's written feeding plan and the child's doctor. New foods, especially allergens, are usually offered at home first.
  • It can take 8 to 15 tries before a baby accepts a new taste. "Not yet" is normal.

Allergens

  • Offer one new allergen at a time, early in the day, and watch for about 2 hours.
  • Wait 2 to 3 days before the next new allergen so a reaction is easy to trace.
  • Once tolerated, keep it in the diet a few times a week. The tracker's 3 dots mark those first exposures.

Choking safety

  • Cut round foods like grapes and blueberries into quarters or smash them.
  • No whole nuts, popcorn, hard raw vegetables, or chunks of hot dog.
  • Serve nut butters thinned, never by the spoonful. Always seat babies upright and stay with them.

Wait until 12 months

  • Honey, in any form, including baked goods.
  • Cow's milk as a drink. Yogurt and cheese are fine earlier.
  • Juice. Offer breast milk, formula, and small sips of water instead.

Call 911 right away for

  • Trouble breathing, wheezing, or repeated coughing
  • Swelling of the lips, tongue, or face
  • Repeated vomiting, or a baby who turns pale or floppy

Mild signs to note and share

  • Hives or a rash around the mouth
  • One episode of vomiting or loose stool
  • Tell the family the same day and write it here.

General guidance only, not medical advice. Always check with the child's doctor and the family, especially if a baby has eczema or a known allergy.
